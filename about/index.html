About

A floor to start the next thing from.

floa.one means floor one: the ground floor, where every building starts.

I spent 20+ years working my way up, floor by floor: an ad agency, then games, then consumer software, then enterprise platforms built for the Fortune 500. Each level taught me something the last one could not.

Image

Who I am

I do my best work when a product has outgrown the way it is built: too many teams, too many surfaces, no shared system holding them together. That is where I have spent most of my career, across 15+ launches, a platform carried to the cloud, and a design system teams across the company came to rely on.

Good design is less about polish than clarity: systems that help people focus, decide well, and stay connected.

How I lead

I make the work clear, visible, and owned. I resolve ambiguity before it reaches the team, keep capacity and priorities in plain view, and bring design into roadmap planning from the start. Every designer has a problem space to own and a plan to grow, and product managers describe them as the ones who pull teams together.

Clear the path, protect the autonomy, hold the bar, and grow people until they don't need me.

The story

Where it started

The work began with making things by hand, then making them for other people. Art first, where I learned that an idea is only worth as much as the form you can give it. Then client service, directing creative work for others. Making something land for someone else is a different craft from making it please me, and it taught me to listen for the problem beneath the brief: the one the client could feel but had not named.

Somewhere in there, the making met technology, and the work changed shape. It stopped being about objects and started being about systems, the structures behind what people actually touch. The canvas kept getting bigger and less visible. The task never did: take something formless and give it a structure that holds.

The work underneath

What stayed true across every floor is that the screen is never the product. The interface is visible. The structures that enable it are not. The work that decides whether a product holds together happens underneath, in the systems, the platforms, the operating models, the connections that let teams and products move as one thing instead of many. That is the layer I kept returning to, and over time it became the thing I do best: finding the real problem while everyone is still solving the visible one.

Why this site

This site is a collection of that work, those lessons, and the principles that keep guiding how I practice. It brings what I learned on the way up back to the ground floor, where good foundations start.
