The Spine
What ties the chapters together

Across these chapters, what I was accountable for kept widening: a platform's direction, then a system a whole organization depended on, then an enterprise platform, from the product down to its components. The work stayed the same. The reach kept growing.

AI in the Work
Built for a product where one wrong figure is a regulatory finding

AI enters a regulated product on different terms. Every claim needs evidence it can point to, and a gap has to be flagged, never guessed at. That constraint shaped each piece of AI work I lead.

Agentic reporting

An assembly line, not a chatbot

I set the frame for how AI agents generate regulated reports: specialized agents, each with one job, passing work down the line, with governance set once and inherited by every stage. The workflow now runs inside the process layer my team owns.

AI assistance

Catching problems before a filing does

My team designs AI assistance that works alongside a financial report while it is being built: document health, a security dashboard, data-accuracy checks, and support for the many teams building it together, so issues surface during the work instead of after the report is filed.

Development automation

Moving a legacy platform onto the system

AI automation that converts legacy UI onto the shared design system, instead of rebuilding it screen by screen. Alongside it, I led a rearchitecture of the navigation to the standard people expect from modern productivity suites.

Global Experience
Design that works in every market

Global was a condition of the work from the first day. A design that only works in one market is not finished.

Apple / Claris

One platform, 16 countries

At Claris, I led design across 16 countries and 12+ languages, treating language, input method, and regional workflow as constraints from the first sketch, so decisions held in every market.

Yahoo!

One navigation, global markets

At Yahoo!, I led a global navigation revamp across desktop and mobile, tuned to each region's languages and habits, so one model could serve every market without flattening local differences.

Languages

Three languages, many regions

I speak English, Japanese, and Mandarin, and work with teams across continents and regions. Moving between them is how the work across markets actually got done.
